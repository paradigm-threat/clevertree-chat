Internet as a Forest

A forest grows itself. Roots trade water and sugar; fallen wood feeds the next stand. Crowns open where light arrives and thicken where it does not. Through drought, frost, and fire, the canopy adapts and finds a balance no single tree could hold alone.

That picture is the internet we didn’t get: a canopy of distinct trees on one hill, not ducts. The internet should be allowed to grow organically, without corporate constraints, and still follow rules that keep everything fair, honest, and auditable by the public.

It never grew like this

The internet as we know it today did not grow organically. It is even more constrained than a bonsai tree. And we never got to see its true potential.

That potential is right around the corner because of SI (Super Intelligence, i.e. AI). SI is the better word for what this software is and does: accessibility — making things easy for humans who exist today, including the sick and the disabled — not inventing a new kind of thinking life form. Accessibility kept getting lost in the for-profit, monopolized software industry. Even companies like Apple that seemed to care, and had a head start, abandoned it after a while.

It channeled

The internet we have did not grow at all. It channeled. It became a series of channels like television that corporations can control, predict, and lock down with intellectual property — and most importantly, block competition. Competition would mean facing their own product quality and raising a bar they set as low as it could go. They don’t even care how products are discarded; that’s why we have the big landfills. If governments changed a few things we’d have better products, but governments were never interested.

So we got a channeled, constrained internet. It hasn’t grown like a forest. It has grown like interlocking ducts — like in the movie Brazil — and became unmanageable.

What they sold vs what SI is

They tried to sell us on the idea that we needed AI to manage all of it. SI will help clean that mess up. But the real nature of SI is to make your dreams, your ideas, your thoughts come alive with minimal human effort — without tedious typing and good posture at a workable computer. The future is now a choice between having the corporations manage every last thing in our lives, or doing it ourselves with our own tools, our own SI, home grown, home run.

One or two of us

Clevertree Chat, like the rest of our products, is going to use SI to compete with the corporations. Now we have the abilities they have with all their people — and we can do what they can do with just one or two of us. Two at least: you need human feedback that will never be replaceable. With two or more you can make a product better than what the corporations put out. Faster. Without a salary. Without overhead. Scatter it on the free internet.

Of course the internet is not free yet. It’s locked down, and competing on advertising alone is extremely hard. A lot of this waits until the internet opens enough for the individual to be heard. We’re taking a major step toward that by changing the way people debate.

Everyone talking, no one listening

A debate can rage with thousands lobbing the pro and anti-establishment positions back and forth, and a third voice — a third opinion that was always there — might never enter the conversation. Lost in the flood of comments and threads. Downvoted, shadow-banned, hidden from the average person on the page.

The Fox News message board is a perfect example. It ran for decades. If you ever tried to use it, you’d see a huge flow of comments. Make a comment and it’s gone within seconds. Everyone was talking and no one was listening.

Clevertree Chat aims to let that one person enter the conversation and be heard — simply because they are providing a unique perspective the SI curator has not already assessed. That’s what will change the debate.

We the people will be the cloud

There is no doubt we’re going to move in this direction now. Bold, based vision sees nothing but outrage and chaos. And it failed to prove it has what it takes to be part of the final internet.

In the next version, we the people will be the cloud — and we are all the cloud we’ll ever need.
