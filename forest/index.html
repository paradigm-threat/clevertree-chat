Internet as a Forest

Trees grow together on one horizon — smaller ones farther back and higher on the hill, taller crowns in front. Tall habits reach with long limbs; wide habits stay short and bushy. Each tree draws in over time (no wind sway).

This picture is the internet we didn’t get: a canopy of distinct trees on one hill — not ducts. The argument is that it should be allowed to grow organically, without those constraints, and still follow rules that make everything fair, honest, and auditable by the general public.

It never grew like this

The internet as we know it today did not grow in an organic fashion. This is even more constrained than a bonsai tree. And we never got to see its true potential.

That potential is right around the corner because of SI (Super Intelligence, i.e. AI). SI is the better word for what this software is and does: accessibility, making things easy for humans who exist today — including the sick and the disabled — not inventing a new kind of thinking life form. Accessibility kept getting lost in the for-profit, monopolized software industry. Even companies like Apple that seemed to care, and had a head start, abandoned it after a certain time.

It channeled

The internet we have did not grow at all. It channeled. It became a series of channels like television that corporations can control and predict and maintain intellectual property over — and most importantly, prevent competition. Competition would mean looking at their own product quality and raising a bar they set at the absolute lowest possible. They don’t even care how products are discarded; that’s why we have the big landfills. If governments changed a few things we’d have high-quality products. Governments were never interested.

So we got a channeled, constrained internet. It hasn’t grown like a forest. It’s grown like a series of interlocking ducts — like in the movie Brazil — and became unmanageable.

What they sold vs what SI is

They tried to sell us on the notion that we needed AI to manage all of it. SI will help clean that mess up. But the true nature of SI is to make your dreams, your ideas, your thoughts come alive with minimal human effort — without tedious input and good posture on a workable computer. Voice and video. Soon enough anyway.

One or two of us

Clevertree Chat, like the rest of our products, is going to use SI to compete with the corporations. Now we have the abilities they have with all their people — and we can do what they can do with just one or two of us. Two at least: you need human feedback that will never be replaceable. With two or more you can make a product better than what the corporations put out. Faster. Without a salary. Without overhead. Scatter it on the free internet.

Of course the internet is not free yet. It’s locked down, and competing on advertising alone is extremely hard. A lot of this waits until the internet frees up enough for the individual to be heard. We’re taking a major step toward that by changing the way people debate.

Everyone talking, no one listening

A debate can rage with thousands lobbing the pro and anti-establishment positions back and forth — and a third voice, a third opinion that was always there, might never enter the conversation. Lost in the flood of comments and threads. Downvoted, shadow-banned, hidden from the average person on the page.

The Fox News message board is a perfect example. Live for decades. If you ever tried to use it, you’d see a huge flow of comments. Make a comment and it’s gone within seconds. Everyone was talking and no one was listening.

Clevertree Chat aims to let that one person enter the conversation and be heard — simply because they are providing a unique perspective that hasn’t already been assessed by the SI curator. That’s what will change the debate.

We the people will be the cloud

There is no doubt we’re going to move in this direction now. Bold, based vision is seeing nothing but outrages and chaos. And it failed to prove that it has what it takes to be part of the final internet.

In the next version, we the people will be the cloud — and we are all the cloud that we’ll ever need.

Clevertree Chat, called Cleverchat for short. The name can change. The debates stay.
